        Format, read out of the Dec 2025 template itself: date right-aligned as
        1 July 2026 (no leading zero, month spelled out); SUBJECT: followed by two
        spaces; numbered paragraphs with two spaces after the period; no //SIGNED//.
        The signature block is the part the template calls the correct signature box: it indents
        3.5" from the left margin and sits about five blank lines below the last paragraph.
        Apply the template's built-in Signature style rather than spacing it over by hand — pasting
        pre-indented text into a styled paragraph double-indents it. An Attachments: line goes last,
        back at the left margin.
        
Two deliberate departures from the template's placeholder text:
        it prints USAF and a two-line block, but Reserve members sign USAFR and the
        signed 39 APS examples carry the full four-line block (name, duty title, squadron and wing, base).
        The templates below follow the signed examples.
      

      
    
        
  • LOC (Letter of Counseling) — lowest-level, most common. Documents a specific issue and puts the member on notice. Coaching record, not punishment.
    • 
        
  • LOR (Letter of Reprimand) — formal administrative action, one step up. Carries appeal rights language; normally commander-signed. Get legal eyes on it first.
    • 
        
  • MFR (Memorandum for Record) — no addressee. Documents an event or conversation for the record. Use it to build the paper trail before an LOC/LOR becomes necessary.
    • 
      

    


    

      
      
      
    


    

      

        
Letter of Counseling

        
[SEAL — DEPARTMENT OF THE AIR FORCE / AIR FORCE RESERVE COMMAND — from letterhead template]

                                                              [Day Month Year]

MEMORANDUM FOR [RANK] [LAST NAME], [FIRST NAME]

FROM:  [YOUR OFFICE SYMBOL — e.g. 39 APS/TROP (Pax), TROCR (Ramp), TROCA (Cargo), TROCS (JI), TROAA (ATOC), TROOM (Load Planning)]

SUBJECT:  Letter of Counseling

1.  This letter is to counsel you concerning [specific issue — e.g., "your failure to report for scheduled duty on 14 July 2026"]. This is considered a [performance/conduct] issue that must be corrected.

2.  Specifically, on [date], [factual, observable description of what happened — no interpretation, no character judgments. Example: "you arrived 45 minutes after your scheduled report time of 0700 without prior notification to your supervisor."]. This [is a violation of / does not meet] [cite standard if applicable — AFI, unit policy, published schedule, etc.].

3.  [Optional — only if there is prior relevant history: "This is not the first instance of a similar issue. On (date), you were verbally counseled regarding (same or related issue)."]

4.  You are expected to [specific corrective expectation — e.g., "report for all scheduled duty at the assigned time, and to notify your supervisor in advance of any conflict"]. Failure to correct this behavior may result in further administrative or disciplinary action, to include a Letter of Reprimand.

5.  This is considered administrative counseling and is not a punitive action under the UCMJ. This letter will be filed in [locally / not filed — state your unit's practice] and is not part of your permanent record unless further action is warranted.

6.  If you have questions regarding this counseling, contact me at [phone/email].


[FULL NAME IN CAPS], [Rank], USAFR
[Duty title]
39th Aerial Port Squadron, 302nd Airlift Wing
Peterson Space Force Base, Colo.

Attachments:
[list, or delete this line if none]


I acknowledge receipt of this Letter of Counseling. My signature does not necessarily indicate agreement with its contents.

_____________________________          _______________
[RANK] [NAME], Member                   Date

      

      

        Coaching notes
        
    
          
  • Keep paragraph 2 to facts a video camera could confirm — dates, times, what was and wasn't done. "Was disrespectful" is an argument; "raised his voice and told the supervisor 'this is BS' in front of two other Airmen" is a fact.
    • 
          
  • Always give the member a path to correct it (para 4) — an LOC with no stated expectation reads as punitive, not corrective, and is easier to grieve.
    • 
          
  • Confirm your unit's actual filing practice for LOCs (some units keep none, some keep a flight-level log) before writing para 5 — don't promise "not filed" if your squadron does file them locally.
    • 
        

      

    


    

      

        
Letter of Reprimand

        
[SEAL — DEPARTMENT OF THE AIR FORCE / AIR FORCE RESERVE COMMAND — from letterhead template]

                                                              [Day Month Year]

MEMORANDUM FOR [RANK] [LAST NAME], [FIRST NAME]

FROM:  [OFFICE SYMBOL — normally the commander's, e.g. 39 APS/CC]

SUBJECT:  Letter of Reprimand

1.  You are hereby reprimanded for [specific misconduct or performance failure].

2.  Specifically, on [date(s)], [detailed factual account — what happened, where, who else was present or affected, and how it came to your attention]. This conduct [violates / fails to meet] [cite the specific standard: AFI number, UTA attendance policy, duty performance standard, etc.].

3.  [If applicable: prior corrective action already taken — "You were previously counseled in writing on (date) regarding a similar issue (reference LOC), and were informed at that time that continued failure to (standard) could result in further action."]

4.  Your conduct reflects a disregard for [the standards expected of a member of your grade / mission readiness / good order and discipline — pick what fits]. This is unacceptable and will not be tolerated.

5.  You are required to immediately correct this behavior. Any recurrence may result in more severe administrative action, including but not limited to placement on a Control Roster, referral performance report, or discharge/separation action.

6.  This Letter of Reprimand may be filed in your Unfavorable Information File (UIF) at the discretion of your commander. You have the right to submit a written rebuttal within [duty's standard window — typically 3 duty days, confirm with MPF/legal] of receipt of this letter. Any rebuttal will be attached to and considered with this letter.

7.  This is an administrative action and is separate from and does not preclude any action under the Uniform Code of Military Justice.


[COMMANDER'S NAME IN CAPS], [Rank], USAF
Commander, 39th Aerial Port Squadron
302nd Airlift Wing
Peterson Space Force Base, Colo.

Attachments:
[list, or delete this line if none]


I acknowledge receipt of this Letter of Reprimand on the date indicated below. My signature does not necessarily indicate agreement with its contents. I understand I have the right to submit a written rebuttal within the timeframe stated above.

_____________________________          _______________
[RANK] [NAME], Member                   Date

      

      

        Coaching notes
        
    
          
  • An LOR is normally issued by the commander, not the Flight Chief — your role is usually building the supporting package (facts, dates, prior counseling record, witness statements) and recommending the action up the chain, not signing it yourself. Confirm who actually signs before using this as final.
    • 
          
  • Get legal (SJA) review before this goes out if there's any UIF/control roster/discharge implication downstream — an LOR done wrong is exactly the kind of thing that gets overturned on appeal and makes the unit look worse than doing nothing.
    • 
          
  • Never skip the rebuttal-rights paragraph (6) — omitting it is a due-process defect that alone can get the whole action thrown out.
    • 
        

      

    


    

      

        
Memorandum for Record

        
[SEAL — DEPARTMENT OF THE AIR FORCE / AIR FORCE RESERVE COMMAND — from letterhead template]

                                                              [Day Month Year]

MEMORANDUM FOR RECORD

FROM:  [YOUR OFFICE SYMBOL — e.g. 39 APS/TROP (Pax), TROCR (Ramp), TROCA (Cargo), TROCS (JI), TROAA (ATOC), TROOM (Load Planning)]

SUBJECT:  [Short, specific subject line — e.g., "Verbal Counseling of SSgt (Name) Regarding UTA Attendance, 12 July 2026"]

1.  [State the purpose in one sentence — why this memo exists, and name the member/unit in full on first reference. e.g., "This Memorandum for Record documents a verbal counseling session conducted with Staff Sergeant (Full Name), 39th Aerial Port Squadron, 302nd Airlift Wing, Peterson Space Force Base, Colo., on 12 July 2026 regarding repeated late arrival to scheduled UTA duty."]

2.  [Chronological, factual narrative — what happened, when, who was present, what was said/decided. One idea per numbered paragraph if there's more than one event to capture.]

3.  [If applicable: any follow-up action taken or planned — e.g., "SSgt (Name) was advised that continued lateness would result in a written Letter of Counseling."]

4.  This memorandum is submitted for record-keeping purposes only. [Add if relevant: "No further action was taken at this time."]

5.  Questions may be directed to [Rank Full Name] at [email] or [phone].


[FULL NAME IN CAPS], [Rank], USAFR
[Duty title]
39th Aerial Port Squadron, 302nd Airlift Wing
Peterson Space Force Base, Colo.

Attachments:
[list, or delete this line if none]

      

      

        Coaching notes
        
    
          
  • MFRs have no addressee and need no acknowledgment signature — they document something happened, they don't require the other person to agree.
    • 
          
  • Write it the same day if possible. An MFR written three weeks after the fact ("as I recall...") is far weaker evidence than one dated the day of the event.
    • 
          
  • This is the cheapest, lowest-friction paper trail you can create — when in doubt about whether something is worth documenting, write the MFR. It costs you five minutes and can matter a lot later if the issue repeats and escalates to an LOC or LOR.
    • 
          
  • Closing with a POC paragraph (as in the real example) is good practice even though it's optional — it's what makes an MFR read as a finished official document rather than a note to self.
    •

Feedback Session Prep Worksheet

        
FEEDBACK SESSION PREP WORKSHEET

MEMBER: [Rank Name]
FEEDBACK TYPE: [Initial / Midterm / Close-out / Referral]
RATING PERIOD: [dates]
DATE OF SESSION: [date]

1. DUTY DESCRIPTION
[One or two sentences — what this Airman actually does day to day.]

2. PERFORMANCE SINCE LAST FEEDBACK
Strengths:
- [specific, observable — a task, a save, a metric]

Areas to improve:
- [specific, observable, tied to a standard — not a feeling]

3. WHOLE AIRMAN CONCEPT
- Fitness: [status / next test date]
- PME / CDCs: [status]
- Additional duties: [status]
- Off-duty education / goals: [note if known]

4. GOALS FOR NEXT PERIOD
- [1-3 concrete, measurable goals]

5. TALKING POINTS FOR THE SESSION
Open with a specific strength. State one improvement area plainly, tied to the standard, not your reaction to it. Close with a concrete goal and a date you'll check back in.

      

      
Coaching notesInitial feedback sets the standard before problems exist — it's your single best tool for making sure an LOC never becomes necessary later, because the expectation was already on record. Midterm is the "no surprises before the EPB" checkpoint. Close-out should be written in language you can lift straight into EPB bullets later — write it that way the first time.

    


    

      

        
AF Form 1206 Decoration Narrative

        
DECORATION NARRATIVE — AF FORM 1206

MEMBER: [Rank, Full Name]
DECORATION: [e.g., AF Achievement Medal / AF Commendation Medal / Meritorious Service Medal]
PERIOD OF ACTION/SERVICE: [dates]

NARRATIVE:
[Rank Last Name] distinguished [himself/herself] by [exceptionally meritorious service/outstanding achievement] as [duty title], [unit], from [date] to [date]. During this period, [Name] [specific achievement #1 — quantify: numbers, dollars, percentage, people affected]. [He/She] [specific achievement #2]. [He/She] [specific achievement #3, ideally beyond the individual's own lane — mission or unit-level impact].

[Optional closing: "These actions directly contributed to [outcome] and reflect great credit upon [Name], [unit], and the United States Air Force."]

      

      
Coaching notesQuantify everything you can — "processed 400 passengers with zero discrepancies" beats "worked hard." Use three genuinely distinct achievements, not one achievement restated three ways. Write it the week it happens; character limits force precision and details fade fast if you wait for the suspense deadline.

[2-3 sentences, quantified, tied to primary duty.]

[PME completed, CCAF progress, additional-duty impact.]

AF Form 1168 Statement

        
STATEMENT (for use with AF Form 1168)

I, [Rank, Full Name, SSN last 4], make the following free and voluntary statement to [Investigating Officer/authority], who has identified [himself/herself] to me as [title]. No threats, promises, or coercion have been used against me.

[Chronological, factual, first person. One idea per sentence. State only what you personally witnessed or did — if you're relaying something someone told you, say so explicitly: "I was told by [name] that..."]

I have read this statement consisting of [X] page(s), and it is true to the best of my knowledge and belief.

_____________________________          _______________
Signature                               Date

      

      
Coaching notesFirst person only, only direct knowledge, hearsay flagged as hearsay. Don't guess at dates or times you're unsure of — write "approximately" rather than inventing precision. This is a legal document; let the investigating officer or legal office own the process, your job is an accurate statement, not an argument.

                                                              [Day Month Year]

MEMORANDUM FOR [APPROVAL AUTHORITY, e.g. 39 APS/CC]

[SEAL — DEPARTMENT OF THE AIR FORCE / AIR FORCE RESERVE COMMAND — from letterhead template]

FROM:  [YOUR OFFICE SYMBOL]

1.  PURPOSE. [One paragraph: why you're publishing this and what it covers.]

2.  PRIORITIES. [3-5 numbered priorities, in your own words — mission readiness, taking care of Airmen, standards and discipline, professional development, whatever is actually true for you. State WHY each one matters to you, not just that it does.]

Coaching notesThis one isn't fill-in-the-blank — it's a personal statement, use the skeleton for structure only. Write it before the board result, not after — a package that shows you already think at this altitude is a real differentiator, and drafting it under deadline pressure after you pin on produces something generic. Boards and Airmen alike can tell a template from a real philosophy.

    


    

      

        
IO Appointment Memo + Report Skeleton

        
PART A — APPOINTMENT MEMO (from the commander, appointing you)

[SEAL — DEPARTMENT OF THE AIR FORCE / AIR FORCE RESERVE COMMAND — from letterhead template]

                                                              [Day Month Year]

MEMORANDUM FOR [YOUR NAME], [RANK]

FROM:  [COMMANDER'S OFFICE SYMBOL, e.g. 39 APS/CC]

SUBJECT:  Appointment as Investigating Officer

1.  You are appointed as Investigating Officer (IO) under [AFI 90-301 or applicable authority] to investigate [specific allegation/incident], which occurred on or about [date].

2.  Your report is due to this office no later than [date].

3.  You are authorized to interview witnesses, request documents, and take any other reasonable investigative action necessary to complete this inquiry.


[COMMANDER'S NAME IN CAPS], [Rank], USAF
Commander, 39th Aerial Port Squadron
302nd Airlift Wing
Peterson Space Force Base, Colo.


PART B — IO REPORT SKELETON

[SEAL — DEPARTMENT OF THE AIR FORCE / AIR FORCE RESERVE COMMAND — from letterhead template]

                                                              [Day Month Year]

MEMORANDUM FOR [APPOINTING COMMANDER'S OFFICE SYMBOL]

FROM:  [YOUR OFFICE SYMBOL — Investigating Officer]

SUBJECT:  Report of Investigation — [Subject/Case Reference]

1.  APPOINTMENT. [Cite the appointment memo, date, and scope.]
2.  ALLEGATION/ISSUE. [Restate precisely what you were asked to investigate.]
3.  METHODOLOGY. [Interviews conducted, documents reviewed, dates.]
4.  FINDINGS OF FACT. [Numbered, factual, one finding per paragraph, evidence cited for each.]
5.  ANALYSIS. [Apply the facts to the standard or regulation at issue.]
6.  CONCLUSION. [Substantiated / Not substantiated / Partially substantiated — state clearly.]
7.  RECOMMENDATIONS. [Only if your appointment memo specifically asks for them.]


[FULL NAME IN CAPS], [Rank], USAFR
Investigating Officer
39th Aerial Port Squadron, 302nd Airlift Wing
Peterson Space Force Base, Colo.

      

      
Coaching notesKeep Findings of Fact and Analysis in separate sections — findings are what happened, analysis is what it means. Mixing them together is the single most common reason an IO report gets challenged on appeal. Loop in legal early, not just at the end when the report's already written.

[SEAL — DEPARTMENT OF THE AIR FORCE / AIR FORCE RESERVE COMMAND — from letterhead template]

                                                              [Day Month Year]

MEMORANDUM FOR [DEVELOPMENT TEAM / PROMOTION BOARD]
