Pre-Screen Handler

Pattern Interrupt Opener
YOU
Hey, is this [Name]?
THEM
Yeah, who's this?
YOU
Hey [Name], this is [Agent Name] — I am a Farmers agent out of [Your City]. Looks like you were doing some research on insurance earlier — I am not calling to pitch you anything, I just had a quick question before I looked at anything on my end.
"I am not calling to pitch you anything" immediately lowers resistance. "A quick question before I looked at anything" signals that you are doing personalized work, not mass-quoting. Tonality: slightly unsure, conversational — not rehearsed sales energy.
Reframe
YOU
When you were looking at quotes — was it mainly about finding a better rate, or was there something specific going on that made you start looking? Like a renewal coming up, a rate hike, something change on your end?
This is not a discovery question — it is a reframe. You are finding out whether they are price-shopping or situation-driven. Situation-driven leads (rate hike, renewal, life change) close at 2-3x the rate of pure price shoppers. What they say here determines which path you take.
Situation Questions
YOU
What are you currently with, and is it just auto, home, or both?
Bundle intel. Both with one carrier means strong retention reason on their end. Split across carriers means major opening — you can consolidate and likely save them money while upgrading coverage.
YOU
And roughly what are you paying now — combined if you have both?
You need this number. If they will not give it: "I am not asking to beat it — I just want to know if I am even in the same ballpark before I waste your time."
YOU
One thing I like to check that most agents skip — do you know offhand what your liability limits are on your auto? Like what you are actually covered for if there is a serious accident?
Most people have state minimum liability — completely inadequate. When they say "I don't know": "Yeah, most people don't — carriers don't usually highlight it." This single question pivots from price to protection.
Problem Awareness — The Coverage Gap
YOU
Here is something I want to flag — and I ask this because I have seen it bite people — with how much rebuild costs and car values have changed the last few years, a lot of people who think they are fully covered are actually sitting on some real gaps. Not because they did anything wrong, just because the policy did not keep up. Is that something anyone has talked to you about, or has it just been a price conversation so far?
"Not because they did anything wrong" removes shame. "Has it just been a price conversation so far" positions every other agent as shallow. They almost always say yes — now you own the conversation.
YOU
For example — on your home, do you know if your dwelling coverage is based on what you paid for the house, or what it would actually cost to rebuild it today?
Purchase price vs. rebuild cost is the most common underinsurance trap. When they do not know — and they will not — they just felt a real gap.

COLORADO: Front Range rebuild costs up 35-45% since 2020. With the fire history in this area, making sure your rebuild coverage is current is not optional.
Consequence + Solution Pivot
YOU
If something happened and coverage came up short — what does that look like for you, financially?
Short. Pointed. Wait for the answer. Do not add to the question. The silence is productive — they are doing the math in their head.
YOU
Here is what I would rather do than just run you a number — let me actually look at what you have and build something that covers the real gaps, then show you what that costs. That way you are comparing an actual policy to what you have, not just a price to a price. Does that make sense?
"A policy to what you have, not a price to a price" reframes your quote as a diagnostic, not a bid. You are no longer competing on price — you are competing on quality and clarity.
Set the Quote Walkthrough — Critical

Do not end this call without scheduling the walkthrough. "I will send it over" is the single most expensive habit in your business.

YOU
I have enough to put something together — it will take me about 15-20 minutes. I want to walk you through it rather than just send you a number, because without context a quote does not tell you much. Are you available later today — like around [time] — for a quick 10-minute call?
"Rather than just send you a number" subtly positions every other agent who emailed a quote as doing inferior work. "10-minute call" lowers the commitment threshold. Most people say yes.
YOU
Perfect. I will send you the quote right before we talk so you have it in front of you — but I want to be on the phone when you open it so I can point out the two or three things that actually matter. I will call you at [time].
"Right before we talk" is intentional — you are not sending it hours early so they form opinions without you. "The two or three things that actually matter" creates anticipation.
NOTE
Tonality reminder: slightly unsure opener, genuinely curious questions, zero urgency in your voice until THEY bring urgency. If you sound like you need the sale, you have already lost it.
On the Walkthrough Call: Pivot to Life

Once they say yes to home/auto on the walkthrough call, you are in the highest-trust moment you will have with this person. Do not let them hang up without asking this. It costs you 30 seconds and the downside is "no."

YOU
Awesome — I will get this set up on my end. One more thing before I let you go, totally separate question: do you have life insurance through work, or on your own?
"Totally separate question" lowers their guard — you are not bundling it into the home/auto sale, which makes it feel less like an upsell. Ask AFTER they have committed, never before. They are most receptive right after saying yes to something.
THEM
"Yeah, I have some through work" / "No, I don't really have any."
YOU
Okay, good to know. Most workplace life policies are pretty thin and they go away the day you leave the job — so a lot of people end up underinsured without realizing it. I am not trying to sell you anything right now, but would it be worth me running a quick number on what term life would cost to fill that gap? Takes me two minutes, no obligation, and at least then you know where you stand.
Works for both answers. "I am not trying to sell you anything right now" + "no obligation" removes pressure entirely. "At least then you know where you stand" reframes this as information, not a pitch — almost no one says no to a free number.
THEM
"Sure, go ahead and run it."
YOU
Perfect — I will put that together along with everything else and walk you through it next time we talk, so it is all in one place. Same deal as today: no pressure, just numbers so you can make an informed call.
Do not turn this into a second sales call right now — you just closed home/auto, do not risk it by overloading the moment. Bundle the life quote into your next scheduled touchpoint (bind confirmation, follow-up call, or post-bind sequence) where it gets dedicated attention.
NOTE
If they decline outright ("not interested," "I'm good"), do not push. Just say: "No problem at all — just wanted to put it on your radar. If that ever changes, I'm easy to reach." Then move on. This plants the seed for a future renewal or referral conversation without costing you anything today.
If They Push Back on the Walkthrough
THEM
Just send it over, I will take a look.
YOU
I can do that — I just want to flag one thing. There is a coverage number in there that is going to look different from what you have seen from other carriers, and without context it might look like I am more expensive. I am not — I just built it differently. It will take me two minutes to show you why. What is a better time — later today or tomorrow morning?
You are giving them a specific, legitimate reason why a naked email will mislead them. "It might look like I am more expensive" is honest — and honesty builds trust. Binary close at the end.
THEM
I really just want to see the number first.
YOU
Fair enough — I will send it now. One thing I will ask: if a number looks off or something is confusing, just text me before you make any decisions. I would rather explain it than have you compare apples to oranges and think Farmers is not the right fit when it actually might be.
If they absolutely will not schedule: send it but plant seeds. "If a number looks off" gives them a low-friction action instead of ghosting. "Apples to oranges" primes them to question their own comparison process.

VM 1 — First Attempt
Voicemail · ~20 seconds
Hey [Name], it is [Agent Name] — [Your Number]. I saw your information come through earlier and I had a quick question before I put anything together on my end — it will actually change what I look at for you. Give me a call back when you get a chance. Again, [Agent Name], [Your Number].
"A quick question before I put anything together" signals customization, not mass-quoting. "It will actually change what I look at" implies their situation matters. Number given twice — people are not ready to write it down the first time.
VM 2 — Follow-Up (Day 3–5)
Voicemail · ~20 seconds
Hey [Name], [Agent Name] again at [Your Number]. I was looking at some info on my end and noticed something I think is worth a 2-minute conversation before you make any decisions on coverage. Nothing urgent — just something I would want to know if I were in your position. [Your Number].
"Something I would want to know if I were in your position" implies information that serves them, not you. They feel like they might be missing something. That is the callback trigger.
VM 3 — Breakup (Day 10+)
Voicemail · breakup tone
Hey [Name], [Agent Name] at [Your Number] — last time I will reach out. I get it, timing is probably just not right. If that changes and you want someone to actually walk through your coverage instead of just throwing a number at you, I am here. Take care.
"Last time I will reach out" releases all pressure and creates scarcity. The casual sign-off signals confident indifference — you have other clients, you will be fine. That posture often triggers a callback within 24–48 hours.
Text 1 — After 1st Missed Call
Text · send within 5 min of VM
Hey [Name] — [Agent Name] here, Farmers agent. Just tried to reach you. Had a quick question before I put anything together on your end — easier to talk now or later today?
"Before I put anything together" creates mild obligation. Binary close makes it easy to respond. No pitch, no company name. Send within 5 minutes of the missed call.
Text 2 — After 2nd Missed Call
Text · after 2nd attempt
Hey [Name], [Agent Name] — Farmers. I was looking at your area and there is something specific about coverage in CO right now I think is worth knowing before you make any decisions. Two minutes on the phone — what time works today?
Shift from "I have a question" to "I have information" — higher value. In CO: hail season activity. Both are real and timely.
Text 3 — Day 2, 3rd Missed Call
Text · day 2 no contact
[Name] — I noticed something when I was reviewing your info that most people in [their city] do not catch until it is too late. Not trying to be dramatic — just want to make sure you have the full picture before you decide anything. Worth a 2-min call?
Drop the greeting formality — just "[Name]" is a pattern interrupt. "Most people in [their city] do not catch until it is too late" creates FOMO. "Not trying to be dramatic" makes it more credible.
Text 4 — Breakup
Text · final outreach
Hey [Name] — last time I will reach out. If the timing is not right, totally get it. If you ever want someone to actually look at your coverage instead of just send you a number, [Your Number]. Take care.
Phone number in the text body so they can tap it directly on mobile. Casual sign-off signals confidence — you will be fine either way. This posture triggers late callbacks when their renewal hits.
Text 5 — Pre-Appointment (send the morning of)
Text · morning of the scheduled call
Morning [Name] — [Your First Name] with Farmers. Still good for [TIME] today? I have your quote built and it will take about 15 minutes to walk through. Two things to have handy if you can: your current declarations page and your driver's license numbers. If today got away from you, just say the word and I will move it.
Send this the morning of, not the night before — day-of beats night-before for show rate. Three jobs in one text: (1) re-confirm so a silent no-show becomes a reply, (2) set the time expectation so 15 minutes feels small, (3) get the dec page and DL numbers gathered before the call so you are not stalling mid-quote. Offering to move it removes the shame that makes people ghost instead of reschedule — a reschedule is a live lead, a no-show is a dead one. If they do not reply and do not show, roll straight into the No-Show sequence in section 05.

03 — Handling
Objections
24 objections — filter by phase
The 3-Step: Clarify → Discuss → Diffuse

Every objection response below uses this structure. Before firing a response, run through these three steps.

Step 1 — Clarify

Do not assume you know what they mean. Ask a clarifying question first. "When you say [repeat back] — how do you mean?" Most salespeople skip this and answer the wrong objection entirely.

Step 2 — Discuss

Once you understand what is behind the concern, discuss it conversationally — not defensively. Talk through it as two people on the same side of the table trying to solve their problem.

Step 3 — Diffuse

Ask how they see themselves resolving the concern. Let them talk themselves toward the next step. "If there was a way you could [what they want] — would that help?" Then get a specific commitment.

Step 1 Clarifying Questions — Pull From These Before Responding

Any of these can follow any objection. Using one instead of immediately defending yourself changes the entire dynamic.

"When you say [repeat back] — what do you mean by that?"
"How did you arrive at that?"
"I am curious — why do you feel that way?"
"Can you tell me more about that?"
"Can I ask where you got that from?"
"Why do you ask that?"
"Can you tell me exactly what you mean by that?"
"How did you arrive at thinking that way?"
The Pre-Close Commitment Check

Before asking for payment or the next step, surface any remaining resistance: "Do you have anything else you would like to address before we move forward?" or "Are we on the same page with this?" If yes — close. If something surfaces — better now than after they ghost you.

06 — Script
Winback Call Script
CSR outreach — former Farmers customers who left. Goal: book the re-quote review, not close the sale.
Pre-Call Prep — never dial blind
PREP
Pull the file before you dial: what policies they had, how long they were with us, WHY they left (rate increase, claim, service, moved carriers with a new car/home), and when. If they left angry over a claim or a service failure, flag it — that call takes the Path B track below. Have their old premium and coverages in front of you.
A winback is NOT a cold call. You have history — use it or lose the one advantage you have. Nothing kills a winback faster than obviously not knowing they filed a hail claim with us in 2024. Two minutes of prep doubles the connect-to-appointment rate.
Opener — familiar, honest, zero pitch
YOU
Hey, is this [Name]?
THEM
Yeah, who's this?
YOU
Hey [Name], this is [Your Name] from [Agent Name]'s Farmers office here in [City] — you had your [auto/home] with us until about [month/year]. I'm not calling to talk you into anything today. Honest reason for the call: [Agent Name] has me checking in with folks who left, because a lot has changed on our end since then — and before I looked at anything, I wanted to ask you one question.
Three moves in one breath: (1) identity + history — you are not a stranger; (2) "not calling to talk you into anything" — drops resistance; (3) "one question" — earns 30 more seconds. Tonality: warm and unhurried, like calling an old client, because that is exactly what this is.
The Exit Reframe — find out why they really left
YOU
When you moved your coverage — was that mostly about the rate at the time, or did something happen that pushed you out the door? I ask because it tells me whether it's even worth your time to hear what's changed.
Everything hinges on this answer. Price-leavers take Path A. Claim/service-leavers take Path B — and if you pitch a price-leaver script at someone who left angry, the call is over. Let them talk. Do not defend, do not interrupt.
Path A — they left over price
YOU
That makes sense — rates across Colorado were brutal that year, and honestly you probably made the right call at the time. Here's what's different now: rates have moved again, and the quote you'd get today is built fresh, not off your old file. Quick gut check — what are you paying now, and has it crept up since you switched?
"You probably made the right call" is the most disarming sentence in this script — it validates the exit instead of relitigating it. Then "has it crept up" plants the seed: the carrier that won them on price almost always raised them since. Most say yes. That admission does your selling for you.
YOU
And is everything about the same as when you were with us — same cars, same house — or has anything changed? New vehicle, teen driver, roof work, anything like that?
Life changes are re-rate events — every one is a reason today's number differs from the one that made them leave. It also updates the file so [Agent Name] quotes accurately.
Path B — they left over a claim or service failure
YOU
I'm glad you told me that, and I'm not going to argue with your experience — if that's how it went, leaving was a reasonable response. Can I ask what would have needed to happen differently for you to have stayed?
Own it, never defend it. "What would have needed to happen differently" moves them from venting the past to describing a fixable future — and their answer is exactly what you tell [Agent Name] before the appointment. If they are still hot about it, do NOT push a quote: "Fair enough — I'll note it, and if you're ever open to another look, we're here." Log it and move on. A respectful exit keeps the door open for next year's call.
The Close — book the review, don't sell the policy
YOU
Here's what I'd suggest — no commitment on your end. [Agent Name] rebuilds your quote from scratch with today's rates and whatever's changed, then walks you through it side-by-side with what you're paying now, takes about ten minutes. If the number doesn't beat what you've got, [he/she] will tell you to stay put. Would [day] at [time] work, or is [alt day/time] better?
The CSR's finish line is the APPOINTMENT, not the sale. "[He/she] will tell you to stay put" is the trust-builder — and it must actually be true. Always offer two specific times; "sometime next week" books nothing. Log the appointment in the pipeline board so the lead lands in the producer's column with the exit reason in the notes.
Winback Objections
THEM
"I'm happy where I am."
YOU
That's fair — and if the review confirms you're in the right spot, that's a win, you'll know for sure instead of hoping. Most people haven't actually seen a side-by-side since the day they switched. Ten minutes to know for certain — worth it?
Reframe the review as validation, not a pitch. "Know for sure instead of hoping" works on anyone who has watched their renewal creep up.
THEM
"You guys are the ones who raised my rates."
YOU
You're right — we did, and so did every carrier in Colorado that year; you just felt ours first. That's exactly why I'm calling now instead of then. The market's moved, and the only way to know where you actually stand today is a fresh number. If it's not better, you've lost ten minutes and gained proof.
Agree first — "you're right" takes the fight out of it. Never argue the old rate was justified; contextualize it (Colorado hail, statewide increases) and pivot to today.
THEM
"Just email me something."
YOU
I could, but a generic number without your current coverages next to it won't tell you anything useful. Give me what you're paying now and I'll have [Agent Name] build the real comparison — then the email actually means something. What's the current premium running?
"Email me" is a soft brush-off. Trade the email for the one piece of intel that makes a real quote possible. If they give the number, you're back in the call; if they won't, send the winback text template and schedule the follow-up.
