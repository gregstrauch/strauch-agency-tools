◆ The Standard: A Life Quote With Every Package

Every quote that leaves this agency includes a term life number. Not asked about — included. You plant it during intake, run it during the quote build, and present it on the walkthrough using the same Problem Awareness → Consequence → Solution Pivot sequence as home/auto.

Pattern Interrupt Opener
YOU

NEVER open with "is this [Name]?" — that is the telemarketer tell, and it hands them the "who is this?" defence before you have said anything. This is a reframe, not discovery. Situation-driven leads (rate hike, renewal, life change) close at 2-3x the rate of pure price shoppers.

Bundle intel. Both with one carrier means strong retention reason on their end. Split across carriers means major opening.

THE LIFE SEED — ASK ON EVERY CALL. Their answer tells you household structure AND coverage gap. This is what makes the life number on the quote feel expected instead of sneaky.
YOU
Good to know. Heads up — when I build your quote, I include a quick term life number with every package I put together. It is just how I do it — takes me two minutes and it gives you the complete picture of what protecting the household actually costs. No obligation on it, but it will be on there.
THE EXPECTATION SETTER. Makes the life quote a standard of your agency, not a sales tactic. By the time they see the number, they were told it was coming.
Problem Awareness — The Coverage Gap
YOU
Here is something I want to flag — and I ask this because I have seen it bite people — with how much rebuild costs and car values have changed the last few years, a lot of people who think they are fully covered are actually sitting on some real gaps. Not because they did anything wrong, just because the policy did not keep up. Is that something anyone has talked to you about, or has it just been a price conversation so far?
"Not because they did anything wrong" removes shame. They almost always say yes — now you own the conversation.
YOU
For example — on your home, do you know if your dwelling coverage is based on what you paid for the house, or what it would actually cost to rebuild it today?
Purchase price vs. rebuild cost is the most common underinsurance trap.

COLORADO: Front Range rebuild costs up 35-45% since 2020.
Consequence + Solution Pivot
YOU
If something happened and coverage came up short — what does that look like for you, financially?
Short. Pointed. Wait for the answer. The silence is productive.
YOU
Here is what I would rather do than just run you a number — let me actually look at what you have and build something that covers the real gaps, then show you what that costs. That way you are comparing an actual policy to what you have, not just a price to a price. Does that make sense?
Reframes your quote as a diagnostic, not a bid.

Do not end this call without scheduling the walkthrough.

YOU

"10-minute call" lowers the commitment threshold. Most people say yes.
YOU
Perfect. I will send you the quote right before we talk so you have it in front of you — but I want to be on the phone when you open it so I can point out the two or three things that actually matter. I will call you at [time].
"Right before we talk" is intentional.
NOTE
Tonality reminder: slightly unsure opener, genuinely curious questions, zero urgency until THEY bring it.
On the Walkthrough: Problem Awareness on Life

The life quote is already on the package. Do not just announce the number — walk the same Problem Awareness → Consequence → Solution Pivot sequence you use for home/auto.

YOU
Before I let you go — one more thing, back to what you told me about life insurance. If it is through work, quick gut check: do you know how much that actually pays out, and whether it follows you if you ever leave that job?
PROBLEM AWARENESS. Mirrors the dwelling coverage question. People almost never know their group life multiple or that it is not portable.
YOU
And if that job ever changed — got laid off, switched companies, whatever — and something happened to you in that gap with no coverage in place, what does that actually look like for the household?
CONSEQUENCE. Wait for the answer. This is what turns it from "he's selling me life insurance" into "I don't have a plan for this."
YOU
That is exactly why I run a number with every package instead of leaving it to chance. Based on what you told me about the household, I quoted [X coverage amount] of term — and it came out to about [$X] a month. Most people are surprised how low that is.
SOLUTION PIVOT. The number now lands as the answer to their own gap, not an announcement.
YOU
Here is the reality — if you have life through work, it is usually 1-2x your salary and it disappears the day you leave that job. This policy is yours no matter where you work, and the rate is locked at your age today. Every year you wait, that monthly number goes up. Want me to add it to the package while we are setting everything up, or do you want to sit with it?
THE BINARY CLOSE. Bundles the friction — one more yes instead of a separate decision.
THEM
"Let me think about the life part."
YOU
Totally fine — it is on the quote so you have the number. One thing I will do: I will check back in with you in about 30 days once the home and auto are settled in, and we can look at it fresh with no other decisions in the way. Sound fair?
THE SCHEDULED RETURN. Feeds directly into Post-Bind Day 30. Log the details in your CRM.
NOTE
If they decline outright, do not push. "No problem — the number stays on file, and if anything changes with the household, it takes one phone call." A no today is a pipeline entry, not a dead end.

03 — Handling
Objections
30 objections — filter by phase
The Method: Skeptical → Curious → Committed

Every objection below is built on consultative questioning mechanics — the goal is never to rebut. It is to ask questions that let the prospect talk themselves out of the objection. A statement you make can be argued with. A conclusion they reach on their own cannot.

Tonality First

How you say it beats what you say. Calm, curious, slightly-unsure tone — never eager or defensive. Slow down. Sound like you are helping them think, not closing them.

Ask, Don't Tell

Never counter an objection with a statement. Counter it with a question that exposes the gap in their own thinking. When they answer, THEY surface the problem.

Let Silence Work

After a real question, stop talking. The pause is where they do the internal math that moves them. Ask, then wait.

The 3 Question Types That Move Every Objection
1. CLARIFYING QUESTIONNeutralizes the reflex and finds the REAL objection. You cannot solve an objection you have not identified.
2. CONSEQUENCE QUESTIONMakes them feel the cost of the gap in their own words. Logic makes people think; consequence makes them move.
3. COMMITMENT QUESTIONLets them state the next step themselves. A yes they say out loud sticks.
The Pre-Commitment Check

Before asking for the next step, surface remaining resistance: "Is there anything still sitting in the back of your mind before we move forward?" Never assume silence is agreement.

06 — Reference
Reading the Buyer
Adapt your framing to who is actually on the phone
◆ The Core Idea

Everything in Sections 01–05 assumes one prospect. There isn't one. The same true statement lands completely differently depending on who is on the line. This section is about how you frame a true thing — never about manufacturing a false one. Read the person in the first two minutes, then run the same scripts with adjusted language.

The Hard Line — Read This First

Every need below has a matching fear. You will be tempted to press the fear — imply they'd look foolish, careless, or like a bad parent if they don't buy. Do not. Three reasons: it is manipulation; a fear-closed client cancels at renewal and you lose the lifetime value; and it destroys the "if they're better, take it" posture that generates every referral you get. Use needs to decide how you say a true thing. Never to invent a threat.

Part 1 — The Six Needs (Everyone Runs Two)

Listen in the first two minutes. Most people show two dominant needs. Match your framing to those two and the rest of the call gets easier.

SIGNIFICANCE — needs to feel important
Phone tells: Talks about what they've built, how long they've been at something, their business, the work they put into the house. Name-drops. Corrects small details about themselves.

Frame it: Acknowledge what they've built before you talk coverage. "You've clearly put a lot into this place." Position coverage as protecting an achievement, not buying a product. The dwelling rebuild-cost gap hits hard here — it is their accomplishment being under-valued.

Avoid: Generic scripts. They can hear it. Anything that treats their house like every other house.
APPROVAL — needs to be liked
Phone tells: Agrees fast. Apologizes. Hedges — "is that okay?", "if that's alright." Avoids saying no directly. Very pleasant, low friction.

Frame it: Reassure the decision itself. "That's a smart way to think about it." They need to feel good about you, not just the policy.

Critical warning: These are your ghosters. They say yes to be agreeable, then disappear. Always confirm twice and use the Pre-Commitment Check: "Is there anything still sitting in the back of your mind?" An approval buyer will not volunteer an objection — you have to invite it.
ACCEPTANCE — needs to belong
Phone tells: Says "we" a lot. References family, neighbors, community, church, the neighborhood. Asks what other people do.

Frame it: Belonging language works exactly as written in your current scripts. "Most families around [area]..." Bundle framing lands well — household protection, everyone under one roof covered.

Best fit for: The life insurance conversation. Household protection is native to how they already think.
INTELLIGENCE — needs to be seen as smart
Phone tells: Asks technical questions. References articles or research. Corrects you. Wants specifics, limits, numbers. May test you early.

Frame it: Treat them as a peer analyst, not a customer. Hand them the data and let them reach the conclusion. This is the single best fit for your existing coverage-gap questions — "do you know if your dwelling is rebuild cost or purchase price?" lets them discover the gap themselves, which is exactly what this buyer needs.

Avoid: Explaining things they didn't ask about. Over-simplifying. Anything that sounds like you're teaching down to them.
PITY — needs to be heard and believed
Phone tells: Leads with hardship. Claim stories, bad experiences, being wronged by a carrier. "Nobody ever helped me with this."

Frame it: Listen completely before you sell anything. Do not rush to solutions — that is the fastest way to lose this buyer. They must feel heard first. Your existing "I've heard bad things about Farmers" script already does this correctly: get curious, let them finish, label it.

Best fit for: Win-back Day 30 email. They will tell you exactly why they left, in detail, if you actually ask.
POWER / CONTROL — needs to be in charge
Phone tells: Interrupts. Sets the terms — "I'll call you back," "just send it." Blunt. Tests whether you'll fold. Impatient with process.

Frame it: Hand them the levers. Present the options and let them choose. "Here's where I can move and here's where I'd be putting you at risk — your call." Your premium-lowering trade-off script is already built exactly right for this buyer.

Critical warning: Never assumptive-close a power buyer. "I'll get that put together and send it by Friday" triggers rejection just so they can prove they decide. Use the binary instead: "Do you want me to run it, or are you good?"
Part 2 — The Six Decision Styles

How they decide, separate from what they need. Most people run two adjacent styles.

Necessity

Tell: "What do I actually need?" Practical, no frills, but not necessarily cheap-driven.

Frame: "This is the number that actually works — nothing padded in here."

Investment

Tell: Asks about long term, does math out loud, compares.

Frame: Cost-per-month vs. exposure. Your dwelling-gap math lands hardest here.

Conformity

Tell: "Is that typical?" "What do most people do?"

Frame: "Most homeowners in [area] carry..." Your scripts already do this well.

Social Image

Tell: Frames things around family, responsibility, being the one who handles it.

Frame: Protecting the household. Being the person who got it right.

Novelty

Tell: Interested in the app, the tech, how it works now vs. before.

Frame: Modern service model, app, proactive annual reviews.

Deviance ⚠

Tell: Rejects the standard answer. "I don't do things the normal way."

Frame: The smarter non-obvious choice. Never "most people."

⚠ The Fix Your Scripts Actually Need

Read back through Sections 01, 03, and 05. Your scripts use "most people don't check this," "a lot of Colorado homeowners," and "most people are surprised how low that is" constantly. That is a conformity lever — and it is excellent on conformity, acceptance, and social-image buyers, which is most of the market.

But on a deviance-driven buyer it actively backfires. Telling them what most people do is a reason for them to do the opposite. When you hear someone rejecting standard answers, swap the framing: "Most people never look at this — which is exactly why it catches them." Same fact, inverted so being unusual is the smart position.

Part 3 — Elicitation: Get Intel Without Interrogating

People share more when it feels like a conversation than when it feels like a form. These four replace direct questions when someone is guarded.

Complaint Bait · best for win-backs and "bad experience" objections
Sounds like getting a straight answer out of them has been kind of a project.
Venting feels freeing, and people disclose far more while doing it than they would if asked directly. This one line usually produces the entire story — carrier, what happened, what they actually care about — without a single intake question.
Leading Statement · replaces a direct question
I'd guess nobody's actually walked you through what's on that policy line by line.
A statement they either confirm or correct — either way you get the answer. Feels like empathy, not interrogation, so no defensiveness. Use when a direct question would feel like prying.
Three-Word Mirror · when they say something worth opening up
THEM: "...we just got tired of dealing with them." YOU: "Tired of dealing with them?"
Repeat their last three words as a question, then stop talking. Reflecting makes people feel heard and prompts them to keep going. Strongest when followed by a leading statement rather than another question.
Flattery Deflection · reveals real detail
Sounds like you've stayed on top of this better than most people I talk to.
People deflect compliments to seem modest — and the deflection carries the real information. "Oh, not really, I haven't looked at it since we bought the place in 2019" just handed you the entire gap.
Part 4 — Vocal Stress Cues (Phone-Usable Only)

Body language material is useless to you — you sell on the phone. These four transfer. None of them mean lying. They mean a topic just got uncomfortable, which is where the real objection lives.

Hesitation

A pause before answering a simple question. They're deciding what to tell you, not remembering.

Pitch & Speed Rise

Voice climbs, words speed up. Reliable stress signal mid-sentence.

Question Reversal

They repeat your question back before answering it. Buying time.

Hedge Caveats

"As far as I know," "I think," "probably." Attached to things people normally know for certain.

WHERE THIS ACTUALLY MATTERS — THREE MOMENTS 1. "What are you paying now?" — hesitation here usually means they don't actually know, not that they're hiding it. Switch to the coverage question instead of pushing.
2. "Any claims or tickets?" — hedging here means there's more. Ask once more, gently: "Anything else I should know about before I run it?"
3. "Have you decided?" / "Any questions before we move forward?" — this is the big one. Hesitation on this question means there is an unspoken objection sitting there. Do not accept the yes. Run the Pre-Commitment Check: "Sounds like something's still sitting there — what is it?"
Part 5 — The Two-Minute Read (Field Card)

What to run in the first two minutes of any call, before you get to intake questions.

Field Card · the whole system in five steps
1. LISTEN FIRST — Let them talk 30 seconds longer than feels comfortable. You cannot read someone you keep interrupting. 2. TAG THE NEED — What are they protecting? Their standing (Significance), your approval (Approval), belonging (Acceptance), their competence (Intelligence), being believed (Pity), or control (Power)? 3. TAG THE STYLE — How do they decide? Practical (Necessity), math (Investment), normal (Conformity), image (Social), new (Novelty), or contrarian (Deviance)? 4. ADJUST ONE THING — Do not change the script. Change the framing: - Power buyer → binary close, never assumptive - Approval buyer → confirm twice, invite the objection - Intelligence buyer → data, let them find the gap - Deviance buyer → drop all "most people" language - Pity buyer → listen fully before any solution 5. VERIFY AT THE CLOSE — Hesitation on "any questions before we move forward?" means there is an objection they have not said out loud. Surface it now, not after they ghost.
The point is not to memorize six needs and six styles mid-call. It is to notice one thing about how this person is wired and adjust one thing about how you say what you were going to say anyway.
Guardrails

• Read to frame a true thing better — never to invent a threat.
• Never press someone's fear to force a close. It cancels at renewal and kills referrals.
• Stress cues mean discomfort, not lying. Treat them as a signal to ask, not to accuse.
• If you are unsure of the read, default to the standard script. A wrong read is worse than no read.
• Everything here is diagnostic. The scripts in 01–05 do not change — only your word choice does.

07 — Reference
Life With Every Quote
Quick reference — keep this open next to the phone
◆ The Rule

Every quote that leaves this agency includes a term life number. But the number is never just announced — it is earned the same way the home/auto recommendation is earned: Situation → Problem Awareness → Consequence → Solution Pivot → Present.

1 — Intake: Situation Question
Say this
Last one for the full picture — who else is in the household with you, and do you guys have any life insurance in place? Through work, on your own, or nothing right now?
Then, set the expectation — do not sell yet
Good to know. Heads up — when I build your quote, I include a quick term life number with every package I put together. It is just how I do it — takes me two minutes and it gives you the complete picture of what protecting the household actually costs. No obligation on it, but it will be on there.
WHY THIS IS ONLY STEP 1This earns you the right to ask the gap question later — it is not the pitch itself.
2 — Walkthrough: Problem Awareness
The gap question
Before I let you go — one more thing, back to what you told me about life insurance. If it is through work, quick gut check: do you know how much that actually pays out, and whether it follows you if you ever leave that job?
DO NOT SKIP THISAlmost nobody knows their group life multiple.
3 — Walkthrough: Consequence
The consequence question
And if that job ever changed — got laid off, switched companies, whatever — and something happened to you in that gap with no coverage in place, what does that actually look like for the household?
WAIT FOR THE ANSWERThis is the step that turns it from "he's selling me life insurance" into "I actually do not have a plan for this."
4 — Walkthrough: Solution Pivot + Present
The pivot + the number
That is exactly why I run a number with every package instead of leaving it to chance. Based on what you told me about the household, I quoted [X coverage] of term — and it came out to about [$X] a month. Most people are surprised how low that is.
5 — The Close
The close
If you have life through work, it is usually 1-2x your salary and it disappears the day you leave that job. This policy is yours no matter where you work, and the rate is locked at your age today. Every year you wait, that monthly number goes up. Want me to add it to the package while we are setting everything up, or do you want to sit with it?
If they defer
Totally fine — it is on the quote so you have the number. I will check back in with you in about 30 days once the home and auto are settled in, and we can look at it fresh with no other decisions in the way. Sound fair?
If they decline outright
No problem — the number stays on file, and if anything changes with the household, it takes one phone call.
If They Push Back
Clarify first
Totally fair — is that because you feel like you are already covered through work, or just not something you have thought about yet?
6 — Every Quote Email: The P.S. Block
P.S. block
P.S. — Like I mentioned on our call, I included a term life number on the last page. For [X coverage amount] it came out to [$X]/month. We will go over it in about 60 seconds when we talk — no decision needed before then.

Guardrails
